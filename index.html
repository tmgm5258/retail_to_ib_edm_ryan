Executive Summary

Campaign summary · Phase 1.0
At a glance Click to expand

Retail-to-IB EDM Phase 1.0

A 5-email automated drip campaign designed to convert existing retail trading clients into TMGM Introducing Brokers (IBs).

The campaign represents TMGM's first structured, data-driven effort to activate the retail base as a partner-referral channel — replacing the previous ad-hoc approach with a repeatable, measurable journey.

Unlock a new revenue stream for existing retail clients while growing TMGM's IB referral network.

Phase 1.0 establishes the baseline drip journey to prove the conversion hypothesis and gather clean performance data. Phase 2.0 will layer behavioural segmentation on top of proven performance data — targeting specific trading behaviours, account tiers, and engagement levels.

Phase 1.0 philosophy: Clean baseline first, optimisation second. Minimise variables to get trustworthy data before adding complexity.

A 7-day, 5-email drip sequence (Email 1 → Email 5) with 3 intentional rest days between key messages to allow the message to land and avoid email fatigue.

Emails progressively move clients through 5 psychological stages:

  • Email 1 · Curiosity — Introduce the TMGM IB Program (what it is, how it works in 3 steps)
  • Email 2 · Trust — Explain how rebates may be calculated — illustrative ranges by product
  • Email 3 · Confidence — Address common questions about the program
  • Email 4 · Urgency — Quick recap of the program at a glance
  • Email 5 · Decision — Final soft close with monthly re-entry
Clients who don't convert after Email 5 enter a 30-day cool-down, then re-enter the journey from Email 1 — a repeating cycle designed for long-term brand-safe messaging.
👤Entry Requirement
Retail clients who have completed KYC and made their First Time Deposit (FTD), with a 7-day buffer post-FTD before journey entry.

Decision 1 — Why We Exclude IB-Referred Clients
Protect existing IB relationships · Avoid channel cannibalisation
🤝Meet IB Partner A
They're an active TMGM IB who has built a personal network of 20 retail traders they personally referred to TMGM. These traders sign up, fund their accounts, and every time they trade, IB Partner A earns a daily commission. IB Partner A probably has a WhatsApp group with these clients, offers trading tips, and answers questions — they've built a real relationship.
👤Meet Client B
One of IB Partner A's referred traders. Client B signed up to TMGM specifically because IB Partner A recommended it. Client B's source_marketing_campaign is tagged IB-XXXXXXX-STD-USD (where XXXXXXX is IB Partner A's unique ID). IB Partner A earns commission from every trade Client B places.
⚠️Now Imagine...
TMGM's Retail-to-IB campaign sends Client B an email about becoming a TMGM Introducing Broker.
1
Channel cannibalisation. If Client B becomes an IB, they could start recruiting from the same network IB Partner A would have approached — but any new sign-ups now go under Client B's IB code, not IB Partner A's. IB Partner A loses future revenue opportunity, indirectly caused by TMGM's own marketing.
2
Relationship damage with IB Partner A. If IB Partner A discovers TMGM is directly marketing IB opportunities to their referred clients, they may feel undercut — as if TMGM is stealing from them. This is a serious breach of partnership trust.
3
IB network attrition risk. Upset IBs can take their network to competing brokers, badmouth TMGM in industry communities, or stop actively referring new clients. In a broker's business model, the IB channel is often worth more long-term than any single retail-to-IB conversion.
4
Attribution confusion. If Client B converts, who gets credit? Marketing would claim the Retail-to-IB campaign converted them, but IB Partner A would argue they brought Client B to TMGM in the first place. Clean attribution is impossible.
Exclude all clients where source_marketing_campaign starts with IB- — regardless of region or specific IB.

This is added as one of the 9 filter conditions enforced in the Retail-to-IB journey. A client acquired through any existing IB referral will never enter this campaign, protecting the relationship their referring IB has with them.

Specifically, the filter condition checks: source_marketing_campaign NOT STARTSWITH "IB-" (with NULL values allowed, so pure organic clients still qualify).

Note on Refer-a-Friend: This exclusion applies only to IB- prefixed clients — not to Refer Friend clients. RAF clients are retail-to-retail referrals where the referrer gets a one-time bonus but does NOT earn ongoing commission — so there's no ongoing partnership relationship to protect.

The underlying framework: Channel Conflict Avoidance.

In partner-driven business models (affiliate programs, IB networks, reseller channels), there's a well-established industry principle: if a partner brought you a customer, don't market to that customer in ways that compete with what the partner is doing.

TMGM's IBs are essentially commissioned partners. They acquire retail clients, earn from trading volume, and build long-term relationships. If TMGM turns around and targets those same clients to become IBs themselves — potentially pulling recruits away from the original IB's network — we damage the partnership channel that drives a significant portion of our business.

Protecting the goose that lays the golden eggs. Our IB partners are worth far more long-term than the short-term gains from converting their referred clients into competing IBs.

Decision 2 — Why We Exclude Sales-Acquired Clients
Protect internal Sales team relationships · Avoid cross-channel friction
💼Meet Sales Rep A
They're a TMGM Sales rep (for example, William Zhang, internal ID 6001). Their job is to directly contact prospects, build relationships, walk them through opening an account, help them fund, and maintain ongoing contact. Sales Rep A personally manages a portfolio of clients they've recruited — calling them regularly, sending market updates, answering questions, and being their dedicated point-of-contact at TMGM.
👤Meet Client B
One of Sales Rep A's managed clients. Client B signed up to TMGM after Sales Rep A personally called them and walked them through the onboarding process. Client B's source_marketing_campaign is tagged Sales-6001_WilliamZhang. Sales Rep A is their dedicated TMGM contact — they handle questions, share market insights, and have built a personal relationship over time.
⚠️Now Imagine...
TMGM's Retail-to-IB campaign sends Client B an email about becoming a TMGM Introducing Broker.
1
Channel conflict with Sales team. Sales Rep A is already Client B's dedicated point-of-contact at TMGM. If Client B responds to the IB email and starts engaging with the IB program, they're now dealing with a different TMGM channel — bypassing Sales Rep A entirely. Sales Rep A may feel their managed account is being poached internally, even though both sides of TMGM have legitimate business goals.
2
Sales team relationship damage. If Sales Rep A discovers Marketing is targeting their managed clients for IB opportunities without coordination, it creates internal friction. Trust between Marketing and Sales erodes — Sales may stop sharing client insights, coordinating on handovers, or collaborating on campaigns. In organizations where internal trust breaks down, everyone's performance suffers.
3
Potential SO team policy violation. The Sales Operations (SO) team may have internal policies about cross-promotion to Sales-acquired clients — for example, "Marketing cannot cross-promote to Sales clients for X months after acquisition" or "All campaigns touching Sales accounts must be pre-approved." Sending IB pitches without checking with SO could breach internal governance rules that exist precisely to prevent channel conflict.
4
Dual-incentive confusion. If Client B becomes an IB, their focus may shift from personal trading (where Sales Rep A earns performance credit) to referring others (where Client B earns IB commission). This could reduce the trading volume Sales Rep A was counting on from that account — effectively cannibalizing Sales Rep A's book of business using TMGM's own Marketing resources.
Exclude all clients where source_marketing_campaign starts with Sales- — regardless of which Sales rep acquired them.

This is added alongside the Partnership and IB-referred exclusions as part of the 7-condition filter. The filter syntax is source_marketing_campaign NOT STARTSWITH "Sales-", with NULL values allowed so that pure organic clients still qualify.

How this decision surfaced: During filter design review, Sichen (SFMC) flagged uncertainty about whether Sales-* clients should be included. Rather than verify case-by-case with the SO team or risk breaking an internal policy we didn't know about, we decided to exclude upfront — applying the same logic as the IB-referred exclusion: if the client already has a dedicated relationship manager, Marketing should not cross-promote on top of them.

Why "all Sales reps" and not "only certain ones": The filter cannot distinguish between Sales reps who would and wouldn't care about cross-promotion. Excluding all Sales- prefixed clients is the cleanest, most defensible approach for Phase 1.0. Future phases may introduce more granular Sales team coordination.

The underlying framework: Internal Account Ownership Respect.

What it means: When a TMGM team member — a Sales rep, account manager, or dedicated contact — has directly acquired a client through their own effort (cold outreach, personal relationship building, onboarding support), that client is considered to be under their "account ownership." The team member has invested time, relationship capital, and ongoing service into that client. Respecting this ownership means Marketing should not cross-promote campaigns that might bypass or undermine that team member's relationship with the client.

Why it matters in the broker industry: Brokers typically rely on two distinct client acquisition channels — sales-led (internal team) and marketing-led (campaigns, ads, organic). These channels have different economics. Sales-acquired clients are often higher-LTV because of the personalised onboarding and ongoing service, which requires sustained investment from the Sales rep. If Marketing freely promotes new programs (like IB opportunities) to these clients, it can break the Sales rep's service monopoly, disrupt their incentive structure (Sales reps are often bonused on their accounts' trading volume), and create internal team friction. In mature broker organisations, Sales and Marketing teams agree on clear rules about who communicates with which client segments — sometimes called a channel agreement or account cadence policy.

How it applies here: Clients tagged Sales-* in source_marketing_campaign have a direct relationship manager. Sending them a Retail-to-IB campaign without Sales team coordination risks all the issues above. Excluding them upfront is cleaner than trying to negotiate exceptions case-by-case.

Connects to Decision 1: Decision 1 (Channel Conflict Avoidance) protects external partner relationships with IBs. Decision 2 protects internal team relationships with Sales. Both flow from the same underlying question: "Who else already has a meaningful relationship with this client?"

The Retail-to-IB campaign reaches clients who are TMGM's alone to talk to — not those who already "belong" to an internal Sales rep or external IB partner. Respecting ownership at every layer is how we sustain a healthy, high-performing organisation.

Decision 3 — Why We Exclude Partnership Clients
Protect active partnership funnels · Preserve attribution clarity
🌐Meet the Partnership Team
TMGM's Partnership team runs dedicated campaign pages for clients who want to join as IB (Introducing Broker), Aff (Affiliate), KOL (Key Opinion Leader), or Hyb (Hybrid) partners. Each partnership type has its own landing page, its own onboarding flow, and its own nurture journey. When someone registers through one of these pages, they're flagged with Partnership_IB, Partnership_Aff, Partnership_KOL, or Partnership_Hyb in their source_marketing_campaign field (sometimes with regional prefixes like LA- or SO-).
👤Meet Client B
Client B registered via TMGM's Partnership IB landing page — they were interested in becoming an IB but haven't recruited anyone yet (so Is_IB = false). Client B's source_marketing_campaign is tagged MKT-Partnership_IB. Behind the scenes, Client B is already in the Partnership team's dedicated nurture journey — getting onboarding content, training materials, and follow-up from the Partnership team.
⚠️Now Imagine...
TMGM's Retail-to-IB campaign also sends Client B an email about the IB Program. Client B is now receiving parallel messages from two different TMGM teams about the same IB opportunity.
1
Funnel cross-contamination. Client B is already in the Partnership team's active nurture journey — receiving onboarding content, training, and follow-up designed for partnership-interested prospects. Adding the Retail-to-IB campaign on top means Client B gets two parallel streams of IB content from different teams, often with different tone, different CTAs, and different messaging. This confuses the prospect and dilutes the Partnership team's carefully crafted journey.
2
Attribution confusion. If Client B eventually becomes an IB, who deserves credit — the Partnership team's landing page and nurture journey, or the Retail-to-IB campaign? Both can legitimately claim the conversion. Without clean attribution, teams can't measure what's working, budgets can't be allocated accurately, and performance comparisons become meaningless.
3
Internal team friction. The Partnership team has invested in building the landing pages, nurture sequences, and conversion funnels for partnership-interested prospects. When Marketing sends a parallel campaign into the same audience, the Partnership team may feel their work is being duplicated or undermined — leading to internal disputes over ownership and territory.
4
Email fatigue and unsubscribe risk. A prospect receiving two parallel IB-focused email streams from TMGM is statistically more likely to feel bombarded, mark emails as spam, or unsubscribe entirely. The short-term conversion gain from double-marketing is often offset by the long-term list health damage.
Exclude all clients where source_marketing_campaign contains "Partnership" — regardless of type (IB, Aff, KOL, Hyb) or regional prefix (LA-, SO-, Reassign-).

This was the first of the three exclusions we added, originally flagged during filter design as a gap in our initial logic. Our previous filter only checked Is_IB = false, which would miss clients who registered via a Partnership page with intent to become IB but haven't recruited anyone yet. These clients were technically still "retail" by the Is_IB flag, but strategically they were already in a different funnel.

The filter condition is source_marketing_campaign NOT CONTAINS "Partnership". This catches all variants:

• MKT-Partnership_IB, MKT-Partnership_Aff, MKT-Partnership_KOL, MKT-Partnership_Hyb
• LA-MKT-Partnership_*, SO-MKT-Partnership_*
• Reassign-MKT-Partnership_* (manually reassigned clients)

By blocking the entire "Partnership" keyword, we ensure no client currently in any partnership funnel — regardless of type or region — enters the Retail-to-IB campaign.

The underlying framework: Funnel Integrity.

What it means: When a client has entered a specific marketing funnel — whether it's a retail onboarding journey, a partnership nurture sequence, or a Finfluencer recruitment flow — that funnel is an ongoing conversation between the brand and the prospect. Each funnel has its own pacing, tone, CTAs, and conversion goals, carefully designed by the team running it. Dropping a different campaign into the middle of an active funnel disrupts the conversation, muddles the message, and damages both funnels' performance.

Why it matters in the broker industry: Brokers typically operate multiple parallel acquisition funnels targeting different prospect segments — retail clients, IB partners, affiliates, KOLs, institutional traders, and so on. Each funnel has distinct messaging and different success metrics. For example, a retail funnel optimises for FTD and trading volume, while a partnership funnel optimises for recruits brought in and their downstream volume. When funnels cross-contaminate, measurement breaks down: you can't tell if your partnership campaigns are working because retail campaigns may be stealing their credit, and vice versa. Mature broker orgs use concepts like funnel exclusion rules, audience suppression lists, and campaign isolation to keep each funnel measurable.

How it applies here: Clients tagged with any Partnership keyword are actively in the Partnership team's funnel. The Retail-to-IB campaign is a separate funnel targeting a different audience (pure retail clients who haven't yet engaged with any partnership offering). Letting these funnels overlap would contaminate both sets of data and confuse prospects getting double-exposed.

The underlying framework: Self-Selecting Affinity.

What it means: Clients who have already demonstrated a specific behaviour are statistically far more likely to do that behaviour again — and at scale. Rather than trying to convince a random retail client to become a referrer, it's much more efficient to activate the clients who have already self-selected as referrers through smaller programs (like RAF) and offer them the infrastructure to do it bigger (the IB program).

Why it matters in the broker industry: IB conversion from retail is notoriously difficult because it requires a rare combination of traits: (1) interest in earning commission, (2) an existing network of potential traders, (3) willingness to recommend the broker, and (4) enough trust in the platform to stake their reputation on it. Randomly selected retail clients may have one or two of these traits; the conversion rate is typically low. But RAF referrers have effectively pre-qualified themselves on all four — they're interested enough to have taken action, they have a network (proven by actual referrals), they're willing to recommend (proven by actual recommendations), and they trust TMGM (proven by personally trading). In financial services marketing, this is sometimes called behavioural pre-qualification or intent signal marketing — targeting based on what clients have done, not what they say they'd do.

How it applies here: RAF clients have shown the exact behaviour we want them to repeat at a larger scale — sharing TMGM with their network. The IB program is the natural upgrade path from RAF (ongoing commission vs one-time bonus, structured portal vs ad-hoc sharing, sub-IB network vs flat referrals). Including RAF clients in the Retail-to-IB campaign isn't just "not excluding" them — it's strategically prioritising them as the most likely converters.

Connects back to Decisions 1-3: The first three decisions answered "who should we exclude because they're already in someone else's relationship?" Instead of recruiting IBs from scratch, we're activating the retail clients who've already shown they'll do the work of recommending TMGM — they just need the structure and incentives to scale it. That's the highest-leverage move available in Phase 1.0.

Decision 5 — Why KYC → FTD → 7-Day Buffer Before Journey Entry
Earn the right to pitch · Respect the client's readiness stages
🪪Day -10 — Client B Completes KYC
Client B signs up on TMGM's website, uploads ID, proof of address, and completes identity verification. They're now a verified retail account holder — legally and operationally allowed to trade. But verified doesn't mean engaged. Client B hasn't deposited a single dollar yet, hasn't placed a single trade, and has no track record with the platform. Pitching them the IB program at this point is like asking someone to sell a restaurant after they've only walked past the front door.
💰Day 0 — Client B Makes Their First Time Deposit (FTD)
Client B funds their account with their first deposit (FTD) and places their first trades. This is a meaningful shift — they've skin in the game, they're experiencing execution speed, platform UX, spreads, and customer service firsthand. But it's still early. They're exploring, comparing, figuring out if TMGM is right for them. They haven't formed an opinion yet.
🧘Day 1 to 7 — The Buffer Period
TMGM stays quiet on the IB front. No IB pitch, no promotional emails about earning commission, no "become a partner" messaging. Client B gets space to explore the platform, place trades, see how their strategy performs, interact with support if needed. By the end of Day 7, they've formed an initial opinion about TMGM — positive or negative — and they're ready to hear about more things TMGM offers beyond pure trading. This is when the IB pitch lands.
✨Now Consider...
A client who receives the IB pitch on Day 0 (immediately after FTD) has no basis to evaluate whether TMGM is a platform they'd want to recommend to friends — they've barely used it. A client who receives it on Day 7 has formed a first impression, placed trades, experienced the platform, and is positioned to evaluate the opportunity on merit. The same pitch lands very differently depending on when it arrives.
1
Clients can't evaluate what they haven't experienced. The IB program asks clients to refer friends to TMGM — effectively putting their personal reputation behind the platform. A client who's only deposited 2 days ago has no basis to do that. They haven't tested execution, haven't seen how support handles issues, haven't confirmed the platform works for their trading style. Asking them to recommend TMGM to their network when they haven't even evaluated it themselves is premature — and most clients will intuitively sense that.
2
Undermines perceived credibility of the pitch. When marketing pitches arrive too fast after onboarding, it signals to the client that TMGM prioritises extracting value from them over building relationship. Sophisticated retail clients — the ones most likely to become successful IBs — notice this pattern and discount the brand accordingly. "This broker was pitching me IB sign-up before I even knew what my spread was" becomes a lasting first impression that's very hard to undo.
3
Contaminates early-experience behavioural data. Phase 1.0's measurement goal is to understand how clients respond to the Retail-to-IB campaign. If we pitch clients on Day 0, we're mixing two signals: their response to the campaign AND their response to being pitched while still onboarding. The 7-day buffer isolates the campaign's impact on clients who've had a normal onboarding experience — which is what we need to learn in order to optimise Phase 2.0.
4
Risks regulatory scrutiny around aggressive marketing. Broker industry regulators (including ASIC, which we don't target precisely because of similar concerns) scrutinise early-stage client pitching, especially around products that promise earning opportunities. Pitching newly-funded clients on IB commission within 24-48 hours of their first deposit invites concerns around pressure tactics and suitability. A 7-day buffer demonstrates marketing discipline and reduces regulatory exposure.
Clients must complete KYC verification, make their First Time Deposit (FTD), and then wait 7 days post-FTD before entering the Retail-to-IB journey. This 3-gate sequence — KYC → FTD → 7-day buffer — is enforced as part of the 9 filter conditions.

Filter implementation: Two of our 9 filter conditions enforce this entry logic: ID_Verified = Verified (KYC gate), and TA_First_Deposit_Time IS NOT NULL AND TA_First_Deposit_Time ≤ (today − 7 days) (FTD + buffer combined). A client who's completed KYC but not yet made FTD doesn't match. A client who made FTD 3 days ago doesn't match yet. A client who made FTD 8 days ago matches — and enters the Retail-to-IB journey on their next filter evaluation cycle.

Why each gate matters: KYC is a regulatory gate — we can't legally market to unverified clients. FTD is a commitment signal — unfunded accounts are typically low-intent browsers, not serious prospects. 7-day buffer is an experience gate — gives the client time to form an opinion about TMGM before we ask them to recommend us.

What happens during the 7-day buffer: This window isn't dead air. Other TMGM teams can engage the client during this period — customer service for platform questions, educational content from the retail onboarding journey, product notifications, etc. The restriction is specifically on IB pitch content — because that's the category that requires the client to have formed a view on TMGM first. Once the buffer clears, the Retail-to-IB journey begins.

The underlying framework: Progressive Qualification.

What it means: In any multi-step marketing funnel, there are natural readiness gates — moments where the prospect has accumulated enough context, experience, or signals that a specific pitch becomes appropriate. Pitching before a gate has cleared wastes the pitch; pitching after it has cleared is more likely to convert. Good marketing design is about identifying the right gates and respecting them — not trying to short-circuit the prospect's journey. Each gate is an investment the prospect makes (completing forms, depositing money, forming opinions) that qualifies them for progressively more meaningful asks.

Why it matters in the broker industry: Financial-services clients have notoriously complex decision cycles. They need to verify that a platform works for them (execution, spreads, UX), build trust with the brand (customer service, transparency, reliability), and develop enough experience to form an opinion before considering any "second-level" offering like IB programs or managed accounts. Brokers that rush prospects through this cycle often see high early-stage conversion but poor long-term retention, because clients who converted prematurely churn out when reality doesn't match the pitch. This is sometimes called premature pitching penalty or readiness-mismatch attrition. Mature broker programs use what's called progressive disclosure — revealing increasingly sophisticated offerings only after clients have demonstrated readiness for each stage.

How it applies here: Our 3-gate sequence is a practical implementation of progressive qualification. KYC is a regulatory gate (legally required). FTD is a commitment gate (they've put money in, they're serious). 7-day buffer is an experience gate (they've formed an opinion about the platform). Only after all three have cleared do we believe the IB pitch is credible to the client. Earlier is possible but less effective; later is possible but wastes the opportunity window.

Connects to previous decisions: Decisions 1-3 defined who we exclude and why — based on existing relationships that would conflict with the campaign. Decision 4 defined who we prioritise — based on behaviour that makes them likely converters. Decision 5 defines when that qualified audience becomes ready to hear the pitch. All three dimensions — WHO to exclude, WHO to prioritise, WHEN to pitch — are calibrated together for Phase 1.0 baseline integrity. Together they produce a campaign that respects the client's journey rather than racing to get in front of them.

The same pitch lands very differently at different stages of readiness. Our job is to wait for the right stage — not to pitch first.
